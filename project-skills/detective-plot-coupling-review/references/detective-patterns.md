# Detective Pattern Reference

This reference compresses famous detective novels, films, and games into reusable structures for 《深夜热线：直播间侦探》. Do not copy plots, characters, or set pieces. Translate the pattern into live-call social evidence.

## Canon Patterns

| Reference | Pattern | Adaptation for this game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sherlock Holmes stories | A visible detail is ignored because everyone accepts the wrong social assumption. | Put the clue on screen early, but let the caller's emotional framing make players read it the wrong way first. |
| "Silver Blaze" | The clue is the absence of an expected reaction. | A missing reply, missing transfer note, missing approval step, or missing screenshot edge can matter more than what is shown. |
| "The Hound of the Baskervilles" | A sensational explanation collapses into ordinary logistics. | Let comments chase romance, face, or moral outrage while the real issue is timing, money route, workplace procedure, or document purpose. |
| Agatha Christie / Poirot ensemble mysteries | Nearly everyone lies for a different small reason; the solution is why each lie exists. | The caller, other party, family, boss, introducer, or platform can each edit one piece without all being the main villain. |
| "The Murder of Roger Ackroyd" | A narrator omission changes the frame. | A caller can tell only what makes them sound reasonable; the key is the missing action they took, not a fake fact invented later. |
| "Murder on the Orient Express" | Moral responsibility is distributed, not cleanly individual. | Use for mutual-harm cases: several people protect face or convenience until one person carries the cost. |
| "And Then There Were None" | A closed setting and shrinking options create pressure. | A live call can have a deadline: payment due tonight, shop closing, finance cutoff, parent meeting, public post already sent. |
| Columbo | The audience often knows the broad truth; pleasure comes from the suspect exposing themselves under pressure. | A case can reveal the likely pressure early, then make the player choose which ordinary question makes the caller's version tighten. |
| "Rear Window" | Observation is partial; the frame itself creates misreadings. | Screenshots, cropped chat logs, and table columns should have edges that change meaning when inspected. |
| "Chinatown" | Private cases reveal a larger system of power and cost transfer. | A story pack can end by showing the same pressure system across love, service work, family screening, and office procedure. |
| "Zodiac" / "Memories of Murder" | Some truths remain out of reach; uncertainty is part of the ending. | Keep `unknown` meaningful. The host can stop at what tonight proves instead of pretending to solve every motive. |
| "Knives Out" | Family virtue talk hides inheritance and status interests. | Good words such as family, future, condition, responsibility, and decency can become tools for shifting cost. |
| "The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo" | Archive fragments and family silence form a clue lattice. | Repeated mundane records across cases can become the hidden thread: bills, schedules, proofs, approvals, and who benefits from the missing edge. |
| "The Usual Suspects" | Unreliable testimony seduces with a clean story. | Use carefully: make the caller's omissions fair-play and traceable. Do not solve by saying "everything was fake." |
| "Ace Attorney" | Testimony line by line meets evidence at the exact contradiction point. | Every scene choice should test the current sentence, and the final quote-pick should come from a line the player heard. |
| "Return of the Obra Dinn" | Identification happens by cross-referencing small fixed facts. | Let players connect object type, timeline, role, and money/process flow rather than being told the answer. |
| "Rashomon" | Each party retells the same event keeping only what protects their face, money, or moral position. | The caller's version and the quoted/other-party version should each be self-servingly edited in different places; the gap between the two edits is the playable contradiction. |
| "Gone Girl" | A narrator weaponizes public opinion instead of seeking truth. | A caller can phone in not to understand but to borrow the room: they want the host and comments to pressure the other party into money or relationship concessions. Backflow DMs can expose the caller's own monitoring, drafting, or staging. |
| Social-media incident threads | Public opinion flips when one missing edge appears. | The live room can mimic comment-section overconfidence, but the gameplay should reward waiting for the edge, not joining the pile-on. |

## Reusable Structures

### 1. False Solution Before True Pressure

Give the player an answer that feels complete:

- "He lied about being broke."
- "Tony was just flirting."
- "The MBA was the main fraud."
- "Finance is simply slow."

Then reveal a deeper pressure:

- Who needs someone else to cover the debt?
- Who turns private warmth into customer conversion?
- Who uses honesty language to bargain over future money control?
- Who uses process fog to keep opportunity while moving payment risk?

### 2. Missing Edge

A document should not be "proof" by itself. It should prove one thing while hiding another:

- A bill proves spending, but not when the job stopped.
- A schedule proves a shift, but not why a column was added.
- A certificate proves a label, but not how it was introduced.
- An approval screenshot proves a stage, but not payment status or receiving account.

### 3. Witness Self-Interest

The caller's edit must be understandable:

- They do not want to look foolish.
- They benefited from the pleasant version first.
- They delayed a hard question.
- They want the host to validate a boundary they already crossed.
- They want to keep an advantage while calling it fairness.

This is not "both sides are equally bad." It is a pressure map.

### 4. Reversal by Revaluation

The reveal should not add a speech. It should make a previous line mean something new:

- "Only three days left" becomes a pressure tactic once the spending timeline is visible.
- "We are old acquaintances" becomes a sales channel once the membership record appears.
- "I just care about honesty" becomes incomplete once the bank-flow demand repeats.
- "The approval has passed" becomes incomplete once payment and supplier entry are separated.

### 5. True Excuse, Wrong Coverage

The strongest false solution is a real fact with the wrong scope. The excuse is verifiably true — the store really does teach a sales script, finance really did announce a payment delay, the diploma really is from that school — so the room relaxes. The reversal is not "the excuse was a lie" but "the excuse only covers one of the two paths": the script is real but this table has a private column; the delay is real but the supplier refund never runs through finance. The player's aha is a coverage check, not a lie catch, and the false solution stays alive right up to the flip.

### 6. Identity Futures (身份期货)

The manipulation formula this game keeps exposing: a future identity is issued now, and present money is collected against it — 老板娘, 投资你, 一家人, 主责署名. The identity is the credit instrument; the bill is always due today. Within a case, the pattern is one quote followed by one ask. Across a pack, the recurring formula is the hidden thread: surface it only at the finale wall, where four different words collapse into one grammar.

### 7. Fair Unknown

Detective pleasure is not the same as full certainty. Strong endings can say:

- This can be confirmed.
- This was edited.
- This still cannot be proved tonight.

That boundary is more adult than a neat moral sentence.

## Anti-Patterns

- The case opens by stating its thesis.
- The final answer is a lesson instead of a specific revalued fact.
- The caller is perfectly clean until the recap suddenly says they hid something.
- The other party is only a villain, so the player never has to reason.
- A clue appears once and is never used again.
- A material board repeats what dialogue already proved.
- Every case has the same shape, same risk, same material count, and same kind of missing edge.
- The hidden thread is explained before the player has played enough to feel it.
