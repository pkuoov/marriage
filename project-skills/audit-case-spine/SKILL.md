# Audit Case Spine

Treat dialogue as the last layer. Reconstruct what happened, what each person claims, and what the available evidence can disprove before preserving or rewriting any scene.

## Two-layer consistency

Keep the **author's B story** causally consistent. Do not require the **caller's A story** to be consistent. A playable caller may lie, rename an event, change the subject, omit their gain, move agency to somebody else, or contradict an earlier answer because they want the host to approve an action or protect a self-image.

Treat such a conflict as authored evidence, not a defect, only when all four conditions hold:

1. the writer knows which underlying facts are true, false, edited or still unknown;
2. the caller has a concrete protected interest and a reason to present the first version;
3. an earlier word, amount, refusal, consequence or material edge gives the player a fair trace;
4. a player action can expose the mismatch and change the advice, decision or risk judgment.

Do not polish a caller into a reliable case summary. The surface request may itself be camouflage: record both what help they ask for and what answer, permission, introduction, money or moral endorsement they actually want. Treat an omitted actor, owner, recipient or beneficiary as a designed contradiction only when the noun and amount arise naturally from testimony or material. If the caller has no reason to mention the item, do not make the host ask a setup question that exists only to expose it; plant it in a document or action first, then let a player question recover the missing role.

Do not collapse several self-protective versions into one field called “the lie.” For a full-length case, audit the caller's **layered disguise chain**: opening frame, first defensive patch, later minimization or agency shift, evidence-forced concession, and the protected core that remains. These are not required to be literal false statements; omission, euphemism, amount-shrinking and technically true misdirection count when they serve the same protected purpose. The B story must stay fixed while the A story becomes progressively more expensive to maintain.

For this project, read `story.md`, the target case JSON, its chapter and scene records, relevant character records, `plot/timeline.md`, active arcs, continuity promises/questions, the pack QA report, and the generated continuous script. Also read:

- `project-skills/detective-plot-coupling-review/SKILL.md` for A/B story, fair-play reveals and promise ledgers.
- `project-skills/case-scriptwriting/SKILL.md` only after the spine closes, for question design and dialogue.
- `project-skills/story-dialogue-staging/SKILL.md` when changing cross-night staging, locations, NPC actions or runtime fields.

Read [references/case-spine-ledger.md](references/case-spine-ledger.md) before auditing a case. Fill every required section; do not substitute a prose impression.

## Non-negotiable order

1. Freeze dialogue edits.
2. Write the B-story chronology using only facts the project has established.
3. Write the caller's surface request, desired outcome, self-serving omission and reason for calling tonight.
4. Register each side's obtained benefit, future desired benefit, avoided cost and moral wrapper; quantify at least one behavior.
5. Register the layered disguise chain: pressure trigger, surface version, edited fact, immediate utility, fair trace, player test, forced revision and advice impact for every stage.
6. Build the claim/evidence/counterevidence ledger.
7. Classify apparent contradictions without repairing designed testimony conflicts.
8. Write the causal mainline from opening to aftermath.
9. Map every existing scene and clue to the mainline.
10. Cut or repair scenes that do not belong.
11. Rewrite questions and dialogue.
12. Synchronize canon, runtime data, reading scripts and validation.

Do not repair a broken spine by adding dialogue, atmosphere, an NPC explanation, a sound cue or a new document.

## Evidence rules

- Give every claim a speaker or document source and an earliest access point.
- Separate `supports`, `counters`, `limits` and `unknown`. A limitation is not automatically counterevidence.
- Require two independent visible sources for a core player conclusion. Two paraphrases of the same source count once.
- State what each source proves and does not prove.
- Keep observation, inference and conclusion separate.
- Check totals, dates, ownership, beneficiary, signer, sequence and document coverage at row level.
- Do not join adjacent payments into one money path without a tracing source.
- Do not use a later reveal to make an earlier character know more than they could know.
- A character's omission may damage credibility without proving the opposing party's whole story.
- Keep a **single-reveal ledger** for every load-bearing fact: first visible trace, first legal question, first attributable admission, and later echoes. Once a callback opener or fixed line has stated the owner, amount, beneficiary, or result, a later scene may react to it or change its consequence, but may not stage the same fact as a fresh discovery. If the later scene must own the reveal, keep the earlier surface at the unresolved question or material edge.
- Audit optional investigation against fixed continuation. A later fixed question may rely only on facts available on every reachable route. If a daytime choice is the only source for a premise, either make the later exchange conditional, add a separate route-independent source at the seam, or narrow the fixed line so a player who skipped that choice is not treated as already informed.
- Treat quoted aftermath and callback language as evidence too. Text inside quotation marks must trace to an exact earlier player-visible phrase from the branch that produced it; a meaning-only echo must be written without quotation marks.
- Make every absent party answer the case's strongest attributable accusation. A respondent note may minimize, counter-accuse or concede only the smallest point, but it cannot discuss side grievances while silently skipping the unauthorized act, unpaid amount or consent question that caused the case to change.

## Contradiction classes

Classify each flagged problem as one of four types:

- `hard contradiction`: two established facts cannot both be true. Fix canon.
- `designed testimony contradiction`: a character's visible claims cannot both preserve their preferred version, while the B story, motive and reveal path remain clear. Preserve and stage it for the player.
- `causal gap`: both facts may be true, but the story lacks the event or motive connecting them. Add or move one necessary beat.
- `unsupported inference`: the story or host concludes more than the evidence proves. Narrow or remove the conclusion.
- `apparent conflict`: ambiguity, perspective, incomplete access or different time windows explain the difference. Preserve it as uncertainty; do not manufacture a reveal.

Reserve `hard contradiction` for the authorial fact layer. A caller saying something false is not a hard contradiction when the ledger identifies the edit and its protected interest. Never call a detail a contradiction merely because it is suspicious.

## Layered disguise audit

For a multi-scene flagship case, one opening omission followed by a complete late confession is normally too smooth. Test 3–5 surface versions, but keep only stages that pass all of these checks:

- every stage protects the same concrete desired outcome or self-image;
- a visible pressure makes the prior version unsafe and causes the next patch;
- the caller concedes only what the player has earned, then defends what remains;
- the fair trace predates the reveal and the player test targets that trace;
- at least two exposed stages change the available advice or action, not only trust;
- the host never gains author knowledge and never recites the chain as an editorial summary.

When a later statement conflicts with an earlier one, ask “what did this new wording buy the speaker at this exact moment?” If the answer is only “more drama,” classify it as an accidental inconsistency or padding. If the answer identifies a protected benefit and the next test, preserve it as playable concealment.

## Mainline test

Write one plain sentence for each step:

1. What happened before the call.
2. Why the caller calls tonight.
3. What version the caller presents and what answer they are trying to obtain.
4. What first fact, slip or material edge makes that version incomplete.
5. What forces the first call to stop or change direction.
6. What player action can test the gap.
7. What the second call reinterprets.
8. What decision becomes possible.
9. What remains unknown.
10. What later beat pays off the case without adding a new verdict.

Every step must cause the next. If a step only says “then another clue appears,” the spine is not closed.

## Scene retention test

For each current scene, mark `keep`, `repair`, `move`, `merge` or `cut`. A scene may stay only if it performs at least one unique job:

- adds a fact the player can later use;
- strengthens a live false reading with honest evidence;
- supplies counterevidence;
- changes a character's decision or defensive position;
- plants or pays off a tracked promise;
- establishes a necessary authorization or access path;
- leaves a shaped unknown that matters to the final action.

Cut or merge a scene when its fact was already admitted, it repeats a material in speech, it exists to justify an available asset, or deleting it does not alter any later question, choice, decision or payoff.

## Revision order

Apply approved changes in this order:

1. `story.md`, plot, timeline, chapter, scene and continuity records when structure changes.
2. Character knowledge, motive and voice records.
3. Runtime case JSON: claim order, evidence fields, options, callback openers, truth boundaries and aftermath.
4. Adjacency review and deterministic gates.
5. Generated reading scripts and indexes.

Preserve unrelated working-tree changes. Do not edit generated files by hand.

## Validation

After revising:

- Re-read the continuous script from two exchanges before the first changed beat through two exchanges after the last.
- Verify every question is licensed by already visible information.
- Verify every answer adds one new fact, defense, decision or emotion.
- Verify every planted interruption, sound, object and third-party remark is paid off or removed.
- Verify every callback, live interruption and respondent appearance has an in-world access path: who sent the link, forwarded the clip, asked for the reply or otherwise made the stream/material reachable.
- Verify every load-bearing fact is revealed once; later uses are explicitly reaction, consequence or revaluation rather than a second first reveal.
- Verify skipped optional routes still reach coherent fixed dialogue without phantom knowledge.
- Verify the B story is consistent while every intentional A-story contradiction retains a motive, fair trace, player test and advice-changing payoff.
- Verify `confirmed / edited / unknown` remain distinct.
- Run the repository's content generation, full checks and relevant playable smoke test.

Report the original spine problem, the new spine, scenes cut or repaired, evidence boundaries preserved, and validation results.
