Empty or inapplicable fields do not authorize adding facts, blame, a second night, or new twists. These are author notes, not player-facing dialogue. Chain checks:

- Does every stage serve the same protected purpose?
- Does Stage N+1 occur because Stage N became unsafe?
- Does the speaker concede only what the player has earned?
- Does maintaining the version become harder as visible evidence accumulates?
- What changes after each stage: the dispute, an action, credibility, or the relationship? Does that change belong in this case?

## 6. Revision lock

Before line editing, confirm:

- The B story contains no unresolved hard contradiction.
- The A story has not been polished into perfect consistency.
- Every intentional contradiction has a motive, fair trace, player test and later consequence.
- Every registered disguise stage has an immediate utility and is causally forced by visible pressure; the stages are not unrelated twists.
- Each core conclusion has support appropriate to what it asserts; externally disputed facts have necessary corroboration, and verbal reveals preserve the earlier claim, pressure question and revealing revision.
- Any claimed central omission changes the current dispute or understanding; minor omissions need not become twists.
- The first-night interruption has a causal payoff.
- No scene repeats an already admitted fact through performance or exposition.
- Author-known hidden truth is distinguished from authorial unknowns. Dialogue may actively test a plausible hidden route; recap and aftermath reflect what the exchange actually revealed rather than treating the opening suspicion as an already established fact.
